Principal Investigator

Paul Kramer, PhD

Assistant Professor, Department of Molecular, Cellular, and Developmental Biology

U-M Faculty Page (opens in new tab)

PhD in Neuroscience, Oregon Health and Science University (2016)
BA in Psychology and Neuroscience, Grinnell College (2009)

Dr. Kramer studied Psychology and Neuroscience at Grinnell College. He then went to the National Institutes of Health for a 2-year post-baccalaureate research fellowship with Dr. Veronica Alvarez. Dr. Kramer then joined the lab of Dr. John Williams at the Vollum Institute for his graduate thesis on the physiology of metabotropic glutamate control over dopaminergic neuron activity in health and following cocaine exposure. Dr. Kramer obtained his doctorate in December, 2016 and then joined the research group of Dr. Zayd Khaliq at the National Institutes of Neurological Disorders and Stroke where he studied axonal physiology and axo-axonic neurotransmission.

Favorite ion channel: G Protein-Coupled Inwardly Rectifying K+ Channel (GIRK)

Dream non-science job: Gardener

Postdoctoral Fellows

Lauren R. Madden, PhD

Postdoctoral Fellow

PhD in Biomedical Engineering, University of Michigan (2025)
MS in Biomedical Engineering, University of Michigan (2021)
BS in Neuroscience, University of Minnesota (2018)

Lauren earned her Bachelor of Science in Neuroscience with a minor in Computer Science at the University of Minnesota, where she began her research in Dr. Matthew D. Johnson's lab investigating Deep Brain Stimulation and Spinal Cord Stimulation therapies. After graduating, she continued her work in the Johnson lab while also completing a year-long internship at Medtronic's Neuromodulation facility on the Applied Physics and Modeling team as an Associate Research & Development Engineer. In the fall of 2019, she began the Biomedical Engineering PhD program at the University of Michigan, in the Neural Engineering concentration, and started her doctoral research under the advisement of Dr. Tim M. Bruns, focusing on peripheral neural interfacing. She built and implemented biophysical models to investigate the mechanisms by which sensory neural signals are recorded by microelectrodes, and she used both computational and experimental methods to understand how underlying physiological activity in peripheral nerves influences their output when stimulated by neuromodulation devices. She began her work in Dr. Kramer's lab as a recipient of the Biology of Drug Abuse T32 Postdoctoral Fellowship.

Research: Examining the effects of nicotine on dopaminergic axon signal transmission.

Favorite ion channel: Small conductance Calcium-activated Potassium channel (SK)

Dream non-science job: Antique shop owner

Graduate Students

Vinayak Ghosh, MS

PhD Candidate

MS in Biotechnology, IIT Roorkee (2019)
BS in Microbiology, St. Xavier's College Kolkata (2017)

Vinayak began his research during his Master's thesis in Professor Partha Roy's lab at IIT Roorkee, investigating the role of natural compounds as antioxidants in neurodegeneration. He then served as an intern in Dr. Hiyaa Ghosh's lab at the National Centre for Biological Sciences, India where he worked to understand the role of tcf4 in adult hippocampal neurogenesis. He then went on to work as a Project Assistant in Dr. Bhavani Shankar Sahu's lab at the National Brain Research Centre, India where he explored the role of adaptor proteins in affecting dense core vesicle exocytosis before joining University of Michigan and Dr. Paul Kramer's lab as a PhD student.

Research: Cholinergic regulation of dopamine in healthy and diseased conditions.

Favorite ion channel: α6 subunit of nicotinic receptors (nAChRs)

Dream non-science job: Open a restaurant with cuisine rotations

Jackie Seddon

PhD Candidate, Neuroscience Graduate Program

AB in Neuroscience, Bowdoin College (2023)

Jackie began her research in Dr. Patsy Dickinson's lab at Bowdoin College, completing an honors thesis on the neuromodulatory capacity of the stomatogastric nervous system in different species of crab, and the effects of modulators on neuromuscular junction activity. She then worked as a research technician in Dr. Eve Marder's lab at Brandeis University, exploring the role of K-ATP channels in the cardiac ganglion and stomatogastric ganglion of Cancer borealis. Jackie is also an alumna of the NGP SIREN-NURO summer program at the University of Michigan.

Research: Synaptic and intrinsic properties controlling dopamine axon excitability.

Favorite ion channel: ATP-sensitive K+ channels

Dream non-science job: Indoor cycling instructor
